OVERVIEW

The Brief

Design an enterprise energy management platform for MykroGrid — a cloud operations and facilities monitoring tool giving energy teams a single pane of glass for site health, alarms, and interventions across multiple facilities.

02

The Problem

MykroGrid had no platform to offer enterprise clients managing multiple facilities. Without a product to show, closing larger deals was nearly impossible. Internally, our own engineers were managing grids manually with no standardized workflow, no historical record, and no remote visibility.

03

The Outcome

A concept design used to pitch and close enterprise clients — built on real insights from our own field engineers — pushed directly into development once deals were signed and now live across multiple facilities.

THE PROBLEM

The Challenge

HOW WE GOT THERE

Discovery to Delivery

STEP 01

Research & Discovery

Conducted interviews with MykroGrid’s own site engineers — Samson, Alabi, and Gospel — to understand how energy monitoring actually works on the ground. Core patterns emerged: work is physical and senses-driven, troubleshooting is reactive and experience-based, reporting is inconsistent, and there is no standardized daily workflow or remote visibility. Key insight: software should support, not replace, their physical workflow. These findings became the foundation for the Enterprise platform’s information architecture and feature prioritization.

STEP 02

Persona Development

Built detailed user journeys for each engineer based on interview findings — mapping their morning startup routines, mid-day monitoring habits, troubleshooting workflows, and end-of-day checks. Each engineer had a distinct approach: Samson focused on switchgear and inverter checks, Alabi prioritized customer complaints and line tracing, Gospel led with thermal checks and field walks. These three journeys defined the three core user types the platform needed to serve: the hands-on site engineer, the reactive troubleshooter, and the field inspector.

STEP 03

UI Design & AI-Assisted Workflow

Designed the full platform UI — dashboard, alert management, facility overview, and reporting — with the engineer workflows at the center. The future state journey informed key design decisions: a big screen view for arriving engineers to see all alarms at a glance, auto-generated tasks tied to each alarm type, and a daily checklist that builds an operational history over time. Used an AI-assisted product workflow for research synthesis, UX copy, and rapid concept generation, significantly accelerating the design process.

STEP 04

Handoff & Iteration

Prioritized desktop given timeline constraints, providing detailed mobile guidance for key screens where engineering needed more direction. The research phase — interviews with real engineers doing real work — meant design decisions were grounded in operational reality rather than assumptions. Worked directly with engineers Kene and Oke through handoff, iterating based on technical constraints and stakeholder feedback from the enterprise sales process.

THE RESULT

From concept to enterprise product

Designed a cloud operations and facilities monitoring platform giving energy teams real-time visibility into site health, alarms, and infrastructure performance.

  • Built persona-driven navigation that serves field engineers, operations managers, and executive stakeholders from one interface.

  • Now live and in use across multiple enterprise facilities.
